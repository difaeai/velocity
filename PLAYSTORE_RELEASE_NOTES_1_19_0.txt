WHY THIS IS 1.19.0 AND NOT ANOTHER 1.18.0 BUILD. Apple refused iOS builds 17
and 18 in processing, with:

  ITMS-90186  Invalid Pre-Release Train. The train version '1.18.0' is closed
              for new build submissions
  ITMS-90062  CFBundleShortVersionString [1.18.0] must contain a higher version
              than that of the previously approved version [1.18.0]

So 1.18.0 WAS spent: build 16 was approved on the App Store, which closes the
1.18.0 train for good. Every note written for vc43, vc44 and vc45 assumed the
opposite and was wrong — `PLAYSTORE_RELEASE_NOTES_1_18_0.txt` carries a
correction at the top. iOS builds 17 and 18 are dead; they never processed and
cannot be revived under this version string. Nothing is wrong with the binaries.

A FAILED UPLOAD IS SILENT FROM HERE. `eas submit` reported FINISHED for both 17
and 18, because that only means DELIVERED. Apple fails processing minutes later
and tells EAS nothing. After submitting, read ASC → TestFlight → **Build
Uploads**: each row shows Failed, and the red chip is a link to the real error.
Apple also emails it. Do not record a build as "in App Store Connect" on the
strength of a submit that said FINISHED.

APP STORE "WHAT'S NEW" (set on a NEW 1.19.0 version record)
-----------------------------------------------------------
NEW IN THIS VERSION

SOS NOW REACHES THE PEOPLE WHO CAN ACTUALLY COME

Pressing SOS used to tell Velocity and nobody else. It now opens one sheet that
dials police 15, alerts our desk, and hands you a WhatsApp message to each of
your own five emergency contacts with your location, your driver and the plate
already written in. The messages go from YOUR number, not ours — an 11pm
message from an unknown business number is the one people ignore. There is a
one-tap SMS fallback that works with no internet.

WHO IS WAITING ON YOUR ROAD

Book a shared ride that nobody joins and the screen used to show you a
countdown and a fare that was never going to move. It now shows the riders
standing on the road you are about to drive down — the areas they get in and
out, never doorsteps — and what your own fare becomes if the driver picks them
up. It shows nothing at all when the road is quiet, which is most of the time.

RATE THE PEOPLE YOU SHARE A RIDE WITH

You can see your own ride record, and on a shared ride you can rate the people
you travelled with — privately. Drivers get the same record for their own
trips.

FOR DRIVERS

You can verify yourself inside the app: upload your papers, then a live face
scan. Real documents are no longer refused — a National Highways & Motorway
Police permit and both Islamabad Traffic Police licence layouts used to be read
as a CNIC and auto-rejected. Your car is shown back to you once it is approved.
And you can top up by bank transfer, with the receipt read automatically.

FOR PARTNERS

Starter is now a paid plan at Rs 3,000 a month, alongside Pro at Rs 4,500, in
3, 6 and 12-month blocks.

SINCE THE LAST iOS BUILD APPLE ACCEPTED
  - SOS reaches the rider's own emergency contacts. Police 15, our desk, and
    five WhatsApp deep links sent from the rider's OWN number, plus an SMS
    fallback that needs no internet. An unconfirmed contact is still alerted.
  - Riders see who else is on their corridor, and what it does to their fare.
    Every candidate has already passed all four corridor gates, seats and the
    gender rules server-side. An en-route pickup usually does NOT cut the host's
    fare — the tier ceiling means the gain goes to the driver — so the card only
    shows what the server actually computes.
  - Driver verification: papers first, then the live face scan, with identity
    deciding approval. This is what added expo-camera (6c25304, 15 Oct) — the
    first dependency change since build 16, and it is NOT what Apple refused.
  - The document reader stopped rejecting real Pakistani papers. A NHMP
    "NATIONAL DRIVING PERMIT" was scoring as a CNIC front, and
    `document_wrong_type` is FATAL in decideDriverVerification, so a real driver
    was auto-rejected. Decisive markers settle it now, with the real card
    formats in tests.
  - Road alerts for Islamabad and Rawalpindi, window-gated so they show only
    while the road is actually shut, and the pump price read off PSO's own page
    at six every morning (PSO never gets a casting vote — it lagged OGRA by a
    full day on the day it was added).
  - Partner program: both plans paid, Starter Rs 3,000 against Pro's Rs 4,500.
    The tier id stays `free` on purpose. A lapsed plan earns nothing, but A
    MISSING EXPIRY NEVER LAPSES — the partners who joined while the lower tier
    was genuinely free keep earning until an admin stamps an expiry on them.

BACKEND AND ADMIN ONLY — nothing in the store copy
  - Velocity tests itself: a feature-contract registry (qa/features.ts), a
    15-minute agent, a watcher in the api client, an admin Testing page. Passes
    write nothing.
  - Road alerts are proved SERVER-SIDE, because getTrafficAlerts answers
    {ok:true} with empty lists when it cannot read anything at all — a fast,
    successful, empty call looks identical whether the feature is healthy or
    dead. `platform.traffic` is deliberately NOT in CALLABLE_FEATURE and three
    files say why; getSuggestedRides carries the same warning.
  - An admin page for the accounts money arrives in. adminSetSettlementAccounts
    had been live for months with no caller, so config/settlementAccounts sat
    empty in production, which silently broke three things: a commission-locked
    driver could never clear the lock, the top-up sheet had no account to show,
    and every receipt failed the recipient check.
  - Social desk: a second engine that writes without a paid key, Instagram via
    Facebook Login, self-learning from its own page. Locally written pieces
    never auto-publish.
